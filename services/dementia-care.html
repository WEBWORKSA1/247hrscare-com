Dementia & Alzheimer's Care at Home and in Memory Care

Dementia changes what 'good care' looks like over time. Early on, light support and structure may be enough; in later stages, specialised, often 24-hour, care becomes essential. The right plan matches the stage, safety risks and the family's capacity.

Who is dementia & alzheimer's care for?

  • A new diagnosis of Alzheimer's disease or another dementia
  • Wandering, getting lost, or leaving the stove on
  • Changes in mood, sleep, or recognition of family
  • Caregiver stress, burnout or health decline in the family

What's typically included

Dementia-trained caregivers using person-centred approaches
Routine, meaningful activity and cognitive engagement
Help with bathing, dressing, eating and continence
Home safety set-up and wandering prevention
Support through sundowning and distressed behaviours
Coordination with physicians, day programmes and memory care

What does it cost?

Costs depend on hours and setting: in-home care (U.S. median about $35/hour), adult day programmes (about $95/day) and memory care communities, which usually cost more than standard assisted living ($6,200/month median for assisted living in 2025, CareScout). Use the calculator to compare.

Costs and coverage rules vary by location and change over time. Confirm with providers and programmes directly.

Frequently asked questions

When is it time for memory care?

Common triggers are safety risks the home cannot manage (wandering, falls, aggression), needs that exceed what family and in-home caregivers can cover, or the caregiver's own health failing.

Are home caregivers trained in dementia care?

Training varies widely. Ask agencies what dementia training their caregivers complete, how many hours, and how they handle behaviours like refusal or exit-seeking.

Where can we get free dementia support?

In the U.S., the Alzheimer's Association runs a 24/7 Helpline (1-800-272-3900). In Canada, contact your provincial Alzheimer Society.
