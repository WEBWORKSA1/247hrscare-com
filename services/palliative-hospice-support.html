Palliative Care & Hospice Support at Home

Palliative care focuses on comfort and quality of life during serious illness and can start at any stage. Hospice is palliative care for people with a limited life expectancy who are no longer seeking curative treatment. Private caregivers can complement the hospice team with extra hours at home.

Who is palliative & hospice support for?

  • Serious illness with pain, breathlessness or fatigue
  • People who want to remain at home for end-of-life care
  • Families needing extra hands between hospice nurse visits

What's typically included

Comfort-focused personal care and repositioning
Companionship and vigil support
Respite for family members
Coordination with the hospice nurse and physician
Light housekeeping and meals for the household

What does it cost?

In the U.S., the Medicare hospice benefit covers the hospice team, medications and equipment related to the terminal illness, but not 24-hour custodial caregivers. Many families add private-pay caregivers for nights or longer shifts.

Costs and coverage rules vary by location and change over time. Confirm with providers and programmes directly.

Frequently asked questions

Does choosing hospice mean giving up?

No. Hospice shifts the focus to comfort and dignity. Patients can leave hospice and resume curative treatment if they choose.

Can we get 24-hour help during the final days?

Hospice may provide continuous home care during short periods of crisis; otherwise families combine hospice with private caregivers.
