Live-In Care: One Dedicated Caregiver Living in the Home

With live-in care, one primary caregiver stays in the home for several days at a time, providing help throughout the day and being present at night. It offers continuity — the same familiar face — often at a lower cost than 24-hour shift care.

Who is live-in care for?

  • People who need help during the day but usually sleep through the night
  • Couples who want to stay together at home
  • Families who value one consistent caregiver relationship
  • Rural areas where shift coverage is hard to staff

What's typically included

Personal care, meals, housekeeping and laundry
Medication reminders and appointment escorts
Companionship and routine building
Overnight presence for occasional needs
Rotation with a relief caregiver for days off

What does it cost?

Live-in care is usually billed as a daily rate. Labor rules differ by U.S. state, Canadian province and country — most require an unpaid sleep period of at least 8 hours with at least 5 uninterrupted, plus meal breaks. If night-time help is needed regularly, 24-hour shift care is the safer (and legally cleaner) model.

Costs and coverage rules vary by location and change over time. Confirm with providers and programmes directly.

Frequently asked questions

Does a live-in caregiver need their own room?

Yes. A private, furnished bedroom and access to a bathroom and kitchen are standard requirements.

What happens on the caregiver's days off?

Agencies schedule a trained relief caregiver so coverage is continuous.

Is live-in care cheaper than a care home?

Often comparable, and sometimes cheaper for couples, because two people can be supported by one caregiver.
