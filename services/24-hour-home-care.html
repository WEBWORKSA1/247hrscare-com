24-Hour Home Care: Round-the-Clock Caregivers at Home

24-hour home care means a trained caregiver is awake and on duty in the home at all times, usually by rotating two or three caregivers across 8–12 hour shifts. It is the closest thing to a care facility's level of supervision while your loved one stays in the place they know best.

Who is 24-hour home care for?

  • People at high risk of falls who need help getting up at night
  • Advanced dementia with wandering, sundowning or night-time confusion
  • Late-stage illness, palliative care or recovery after major surgery
  • Anyone who cannot safely be left alone for even short periods
  • Families whose own caregivers are exhausted and need full relief

What's typically included

Continuous awake supervision, day and night
Bathing, dressing, toileting, incontinence care and repositioning
Medication reminders and symptom logs for the family and nurse
Meal preparation, hydration tracking and light housekeeping
Safe transfers, mobility support and fall-prevention routines
Companionship, cognitive activities and family updates

What does it cost?

Non-medical in-home care has a U.S. national median of about $35 per hour (CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025). At 24 hours a day that is roughly $840 per day, or about $25,000+ per month before agency discounts. Live-in care is often priced as a lower daily rate because the caregiver sleeps — see our live-in care guide and use the cost calculator for your own numbers.

Costs and coverage rules vary by location and change over time. Confirm with providers and programmes directly.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between 24-hour care and live-in care?

24-hour care uses two or three caregivers working awake shifts so someone is always alert. Live-in care uses one caregiver who lives in the home and must receive an uninterrupted sleep break and meal breaks, so it suits people who sleep through most nights.

Does Medicare pay for 24-hour home care?

Generally no. Original Medicare covers part-time, intermittent skilled home health, not round-the-clock custodial care. Long-term care insurance, VA benefits, some Medicaid waiver programs and private funds are the usual payment sources.

How fast can 24-hour care start?

Many agencies can staff within 24–72 hours for urgent cases. Having a care plan, medication list and home-access details ready speeds up the start.

Can we use 24-hour care for just a few weeks?

Yes. Short-term 24-hour care is common after hospital discharge or during a family crisis, and agencies typically allow you to step down to fewer hours later.
