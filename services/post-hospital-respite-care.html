Post-Hospital Transition Care & Respite Care

The first 30 days after a hospital stay carry the highest risk of complications and readmission. Transitional care at home and short-term respite care help people recover safely while giving family caregivers essential relief.

Who is post-hospital & respite care for?

  • Discharge after surgery, stroke, heart failure or pneumonia
  • Family caregivers who need a break, vacation or time to work
  • Short-term care while a family caregiver recovers from their own illness

What's typically included

Discharge pick-up and home set-up
Medication reminders aligned with the discharge plan
Help with mobility, bathing and meals during recovery
Monitoring and reporting warning signs to family and clinicians
Planned respite blocks — hours, overnights or several days

What does it cost?

Short-term home care is billed hourly. Some residential communities offer respite stays by the day or week. In the U.S., Medicare may cover skilled home health after a qualifying hospital stay when ordered by a doctor; custodial help is usually private-pay.

Costs and coverage rules vary by location and change over time. Confirm with providers and programmes directly.

Frequently asked questions

How soon should we arrange care before discharge?

As soon as a discharge date is discussed — ideally 2–3 days before. Ask the hospital case manager for the written discharge plan.

Is respite care only for dementia?

No. Respite care supports any family caregiver who needs a break, regardless of the care recipient's condition.
