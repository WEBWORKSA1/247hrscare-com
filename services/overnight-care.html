Overnight Care: Awake and Sleeping Night Caregivers

Nights are when many families hit a wall. Overnight care places a caregiver in the home from evening to morning so your loved one is safe and you can finally sleep. There are two models: awake overnight care (the caregiver stays up) and sleeping overnight care (the caregiver rests but is on hand).

Who is overnight care for?

  • Frequent night-time toileting or incontinence
  • Sundowning, restlessness or wandering after dark
  • Recent falls or new mobility limits
  • Family caregivers who are sleep-deprived
  • Post-surgery recovery in the first weeks home

What's typically included

Bedtime routine and settling
Toileting assistance and repositioning
Night-time safety checks and fall prevention
Reassurance during confusion or anxiety
Morning routine handover to family or day caregiver

What does it cost?

Awake night care is typically billed at the agency's hourly rate (U.S. median about $35/hour in 2025), usually with 8–12 hour minimums. Sleeping night care is often a flat night rate. Many families combine overnight care with family-provided daytime care to control costs.

Costs and coverage rules vary by location and change over time. Confirm with providers and programmes directly.

Frequently asked questions

Which is better — awake or sleeping night care?

If help is needed more than once or twice a night, choose awake care. Sleeping night care works when help is rare and mostly reassurance.

Can overnight care be just a few nights a week?

Yes. Many families book 2–3 nights a week so the main family caregiver gets consistent rest.
