Costs & Paying

How Much Does 24-Hour Care Cost in 2026? Real Numbers & How to Pay

2025 national median costs for in-home care, assisted living and nursing homes, what drives price, and 9 ways families pay for 24/7 care.

By the 247hrsCare Editorial Team · Updated 2026-09-28 · 8 min read ·

Cost is the first question almost every family asks — and the one most websites dodge. Here are the latest national benchmarks, what really moves the price, and the payment routes worth checking before you commit.

National cost benchmarks (U.S.)

The figures below are national medians from the CareScout Cost of Care Survey 2025 (published March 2026, 25,000+ rates collected). Your local rates can be 30% higher or lower.

Rule of thumb: once in-home care passes roughly 8–10 hours a day, a residential setting usually costs less. Above 12 hours a day, the gap widens quickly. Run your own numbers with our care cost calculator.

What drives the price

  • Hours and schedule — nights, weekends and holidays often carry premiums.
  • Care level — companion care costs less than hands-on personal care; skilled nursing costs the most.
  • Model — live-in care is typically cheaper per day than three awake shifts.
  • Location — metro areas and high-wage states cost more.
  • Agency vs private hire — private hires cost less per hour, but you take on payroll, insurance and backup coverage.

9 ways families pay for care

  1. Private funds — savings, pensions, family contributions.
  2. Long-term care insurance — check the elimination period and daily benefit.
  3. Veterans benefits — VA Aid & Attendance and Housebound allowances for eligible veterans and surviving spouses.
  4. Medicaid / waiver programmes — many U.S. states fund home and community-based services for eligible individuals.
  5. Medicare — covers part-time skilled home health and hospice, not ongoing custodial care.
  6. Home equity — sale, rental or (with care) a reverse mortgage.
  7. Life insurance conversions or accelerated benefits.
  8. Employer caregiver benefits — some employers offer backup care.
  9. Provincial / national programmes outside the U.S. — e.g. publicly funded home care in Canadian provinces.

Because eligibility rules change, confirm with the programme directly or a certified elder law professional before making financial decisions.
