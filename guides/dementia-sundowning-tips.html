Sundowning in Dementia: 15 Practical Strategies for Evenings and Nights

What sundowning is, common triggers, and practical daytime, evening and night-time strategies caregivers can use.

By the 247hrsCare Editorial Team · Updated 2026-09-28 · 6 min read ·

Sundowning describes increased confusion, agitation or restlessness in the late afternoon and evening for some people living with dementia. It is exhausting for families — but there is a lot you can do.

Common triggers

  • Fatigue and over-stimulation at the end of the day
  • Low light and shadows that are misinterpreted
  • Hunger, thirst, pain, constipation or needing the toilet
  • Disrupted body clock, too much daytime napping
  • Caregiver stress that the person senses

During the day

  1. Get morning daylight — a walk or time by a window.
  2. Keep a predictable routine for meals, activities and rest.
  3. Limit caffeine and sugar after lunch.
  4. Keep naps short and early.
  5. Plan demanding tasks (appointments, bathing) for the morning.

In the evening

  1. Turn lights on before dusk to reduce shadows.
  2. Close curtains to limit reflections.
  3. Lower noise — switch off busy TV news.
  4. Offer a calming activity: music they love, folding towels, a photo album.
  5. Check basic needs: toilet, hunger, pain, temperature.

At night

  1. Use night-lights along the route to the bathroom.
  2. Respond to the feeling, not the facts — reassure rather than correct.
  3. Consider door alarms or motion sensors for wandering.
  4. Track episodes to spot patterns and share with the doctor.
  5. Get help: overnight caregivers protect both the person and the family.
A sudden change in behaviour can signal infection (such as a urinary tract infection), pain or a medication effect. Contact a clinician if symptoms appear abruptly.
