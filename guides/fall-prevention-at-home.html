Fall Prevention at Home: A Room-by-Room Safety Checklist

A practical room-by-room checklist to reduce fall risk for older adults at home, day and night.

By the 247hrsCare Editorial Team · Updated 2026-09-28 · 5 min read ·

Falls are one of the leading causes of injury among older adults, and many happen at home — often at night on the way to the bathroom. These changes are inexpensive and effective.

Everywhere

  • Remove loose rugs, cords and clutter from walkways.
  • Add bright, even lighting and night-lights.
  • Keep frequently used items between waist and shoulder height.
  • Wear supportive, non-slip footwear indoors.

Bathroom

  • Install grab bars by the toilet and in the shower.
  • Use non-slip mats and a shower chair.
  • Consider a raised toilet seat.

Bedroom

  • Bed height so feet rest flat on the floor when sitting.
  • Lamp or touch light within reach of the bed.
  • Clear, lit path to the bathroom; consider a bedside commode.

Stairs & entry

  • Sturdy handrails on both sides.
  • High-contrast tape on step edges.
  • Light switches at top and bottom.

Health factors

  • Ask a pharmacist to review medications that cause dizziness.
  • Annual vision and hearing checks.
  • Strength and balance exercise — ask about local programmes or physiotherapy.
  • A medical alert device for people who live alone.

If falls are happening at night, overnight care is often the most effective intervention. Learn about overnight care.
